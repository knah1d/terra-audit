Introduction & Background

Flooded rice fields release methane, a strong greenhouse gas. Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD) drains the field at least twice per season and cuts that methane while keeping yields. To earn carbon credits, a project must prove the reduction to an independent verifier.

Problem Statement

Practice is hard to prove

Irrigation logs are incomplete; sensors on every smallholder plot are too costly.

Numbers lose their evidence

Spreadsheets drop the factors, methodology version and history behind a result.

Verifiers need a trail

Every tonne of CO₂e must trace back to its evidence and its rule.

Gap: satellite tools and carbon spreadsheets live apart, so reviewers rebuild the link by hand.

Discussion

Free radar can surface AWD without field sensors, but results stay estimates: a field can drain and re-flood between passes.

Freezing gives reproducibility: a report rebuilt later shows the same numbers and evidence.

Blocking beats guessing: gates stop unsupported claims; tests caught a wrong emission factor (1.4 → 0.85).

ML is a second opinion: about 71% of unseen plots are right with out-of-region labels, so it stays advisory.

10

Conclusion

Terra-Audit shows that the evidence behind an agricultural carbon claim can be collected, calculated under VM0051 and VM0042, and reviewed in one system, traceable from field to final number. Anything the methodology cannot support is blocked, not reported.
